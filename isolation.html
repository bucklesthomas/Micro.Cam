Isolation and precautions

Which infections need a single room, which type of transmission-based precautions, what respiratory protection staff need, and for how long.

Room, precautions and RPE follow the NHS England National Infection Prevention and Control Manual (Appendix 11). Durations are typical values from UKHSA guidance and common practice, not from the manual: follow your trust’s IPC policy and ask the IPC team.

Contact Gloves and apron for contact with the patient or environment; dedicated equipment; enhanced cleaning.

Droplet Fluid-resistant surgical mask (FRSM) within 2 metres, plus eye protection if splash risk.

Airborne FFP3 respirator (fit-tested) or powered hood for all care; negative pressure room with the door closed.

AGP: aerosol-generating procedure (e.g. intubation, non-invasive ventilation, high-flow nasal oxygen, bronchoscopy, airway suction). Standard infection control precautions (hand hygiene, PPE by risk, waste and linen) apply to every patient. If single rooms are short, prioritise airborne, then droplet and diarrhoeal infections, and cohort only with IPC advice.

Sources: NIPCM Appendix 11: aide memoire for optimal patient placement and RPE; NIPCM Chapter 2: transmission-based precautions; UKHSA guidance for measles, varicella, pertussis, mpox, CPE and C. auris; NICE NG33 (tuberculosis). Notifiable: under the Health Protection (Notification) Regulations 2010 (clinical notification).
